Maths that other whiteboards cannot draw

Most online whiteboards are sticky notes and arrows. This one was built for teaching mathematics.

Equations

Write them, do not code them

Click a fraction and you get an empty fraction with boxes to fill in. Tab moves between them. Matrices work like a table — arrow keys move from cell to cell. You never see LaTeX unless you want to.

Graphs

2D graphing that shows its working

Curves, implicit and polar and parametric, lines, vectors. Resolve a vector into components and it prints the working beside it. Intersections are found and labelled automatically.

3D

Surfaces you can turn with a finger

Surfaces, quadrics, planes, level curves, contour maps and saddle points, drawn as board ink you can write over. Watch a cylindrical surface being swept out by a moving line.

Statistics

Grouped data done properly

Histograms against class boundaries with frequency density when the widths differ. Frequency polygons at the midpoints, ogives at the upper boundaries, stem and leaf with a key. The formulae are shown.

Worksheets

Questions and answer keys

Arithmetic through to integration and matrices, in multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, matching and comprehension. Print it, or send it to the class.

On the board

Tables, PDFs and images

Open a PDF and it stays a real document — every page, sharp at any zoom. Drop in an image and write over it. Build a table and type straight into the cells.

Classroom control nobody else offers

Coming with live lessons. Every tool on the board can be switched on or off — for the class, for one student, or for ten minutes.

Per-tool permissions

Sixty-one separate capabilities, each off, view-only or on. Five presets — Listen, Answer, Practice, Exam, Explore — reconfigure the whole class in one tap.

Timed grants and timed pauses

Give one student the 3D grapher for ten minutes, with a countdown both of you can see. Or pause a disruptive board for three, and let it come back on its own.

Ask to unlock

A student taps a locked tool and you get a one-line request. Grant it or decline without stopping the lesson.

It collects almost nothing

This is a design decision, not an afterthought.

Students never make an account

A first name and a room code. No email, no surname, no date of birth, no password. There is no student record to lose.

Nothing is sent anywhere else

No analytics, no advertising, no fonts from another company, no third-party scripts at all. Your browser talks to us and to nobody else.

It is a file you own

Save a lesson and it is yours. Google shut Jamboard down and deleted every teacher's work. That cannot happen to a file on your own computer.
